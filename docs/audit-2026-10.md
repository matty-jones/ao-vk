# Code audit, October 2026

Status: written 2026-10-20 on branch `darkness`. What was fixed on the way is marked **fixed** (with its commit);
the rest are recommendations, most important first.

## The short version

The structure from the start holds. There are three layers: the proxy, the D3D7 front end, and the Vulkan renderer.
Each feature lives in its own file, and the native ports sit behind `[Native]` switches. Of the renderer's ~1330
`Device` members, 750 are used by one feature file only and another 360 by two, so the files really are the
features. The build has no warnings, there are no TODO/FIXME markers, every one of the 319 settings reaches the
renderer, and the settings table, ini, presets and console all come from one table.

The spaghetti is in one place: **the state every feature reads about the world** (the time of day, night, storms,
cloud cover, indoors). It is derived again in each feature, with its own ramps and its own constants. The most
visible result was darkness: a dozen factors, each tuned on its own, multiplying into near black (now
docs/darkness-plan.md). The same pattern is waiting to bite elsewhere.

## Findings

### 1. World state derived over and over (the darkness problem's root; done: `m_cond`)

- Five different "how much is it night" ramps: `m_sunPhase[3]` (sky.cpp, 2-14 degrees down), the moon's (night.cpp,
  2-8), the clouds' (clouds.cpp and again in cloud_light.glsl `Night()`: the same constants in C++ and GLSL, free to
  drift apart), the stars' (6-14), and `NightByLight` (by the game's light, used without a sun).
- The cloud cover (`SkyCloudiness() * vary`, with its `vary` clamp) is worked out in 7 places, and the storm's
  weight (`StormSky`, `StormOvercast`) is read with different smoothings in 6 files.
- The sun's height is computed by hand from `m_trueSunDir` in 4 places instead of calling `SunUp()`.
- `m_daylight` (the sun on flat ground) and `NightAmount()` mean nearly the same thing and are mixed freely.

(Done on the way: `CloudCover()` replaces four copies of the cover's formula, and `SunUp()` the hand-written heights.
Both checked unchanged with `tools/look-check.py`.)

**Recommendation.** One per-frame `Conditions` block, filled in `UpdateEnvironment` (see 2): the sun's height,
the dusk phases, night, the moon, cloud cover, the storm's weights, indoors and darkness. The C++ features read only
that, and the same block goes to the shaders in `FrameLights`, so GLSL stops recomputing `Night()`. Keep the
existing ramps' values (each was tuned against a screenshot), but give them names in one place. `rvk/darkness.cpp` is
the first piece of this.

Done: `m_cond` (rvk.h `Conditions`, sky.cpp `UpdateConditions`) holds the dusk phases and every night ramp, each
named and with its tuned span, set once a frame with the sun's clock. The clouds' night goes to the shaders as
`FL.cond.x`. The weather's ramps are live functions (`StormDeck`, `StormFull`, `ClearSky`, `CloudCover`). No change
to the look (`tools/look-check.py`).

### 2. Environment updates hidden in the shadow map (fixed, 42ee5c2)

The sun clock, the moon, the mist, the sky model, the atmosphere tables and the terrain's sun shade were all
updated at the top of `RenderShadowMap`. They now run in their own step, `Device::UpdateEnvironment` (sky.cpp),
called just before it, in the same order. The output is unchanged (checked by the demo's dusk sweep).

### 3. Settings the in-game window couldn't show (fixed, cf9d637)

AOReloaded's settings window skips any setting whose name is longer than 15 characters (the game's option
variables). Four visible settings were longer, among them `RVK_NativeGround`, the native ground's own switch, so
players couldn't change them in game. They were renamed (`RVK_GroundOn`, `RVK_Pebbles`, `RVK_DepthSteps`,
`RVK_RippleSoft`), and the old names are read once from the ini and its preset slots. The only remaining long name
is the hidden `RVK_SandContrast`.

**Recommendation.** Make the limit a build-time error, not a log line: a `static_assert` over a `constexpr` table
of names, or a check in `native_check`.

### 4. Defaults never reach existing players (done: `kMigrations`)

The ini is rewritten with every setting at each start, so a changed default only reaches new installs. The
darkness work needed a one-off migration for this (`[Migrations] Darkness`). It will happen again with every
re-tune.

**Recommendation.** A small table of changed defaults `{name, old default, new default, migration id}`: once per
id, a stored value equal to the old default moves to the new one. The renames table (`kRenamed`) and the
darkness migration are two cases of the same mechanism.

Done: `kMigrations` in `rvk_settings.cpp`. One entry per change, `{id, now, was, old default}`, covers renames, new
defaults and both together. It runs once per id, in `[Renderer]` and the preset slots. The renames and `RVK_Darkness`
are its first entries.

### 5. Three layers of boilerplate per setting (done: knobs)

Each setting needs:

- a row in the table;
- a branch in `ApplyOne` (320 lines, 109 branches);
- a forwarding method in `ThreadedDevice` (165 of them, each an `Enqueue` lambda);
- an inline setter on `Device` (150).

Most are a single float knob.

**Recommendation.** A generic path for plain knobs: an id per knob and one `SetKnob(id, value)` through the queue
into a `float m_knobs[]` the features read. The bespoke setters stay for those that rebuild something. This removes
about half of `ApplyOne` and `ThreadedDevice`.

Done: `RVK_FLOAT_KNOBS` / `RVK_BOOL_KNOBS` in `rvk.h`, with `rvk/knobs.cpp`. Each of 52 settings is now one line:
its name, its member, and what a change refreshes. They go through `SetKnob` and `KnobByName`. That removed 52
apply branches, 52 forwarding methods and 52 setters, about 370 lines. Settings that rebuild something, take
several values at once or keep a game-thread copy keep their own setters.

### 6. `rvk.h` is 3,500 lines; `Device` is one class for everything

The per-file split works, but nothing in the header says which feature owns a member, and `rvk.h` is the file
every change touches. **Recommendation:** gather each feature's state into a struct named for it (as
`m_cloudParams`, `m_weatherParams`, `m_leaf` already are), declared next to its feature's methods. That is
mechanical and safe, and can go a feature at a time when it is next worked on, not as one big change.

### 7. Very long functions

`RenderSmoke` (815 lines), `BuildGrassTile` (416), `CreateCloudResources` (403), `DrawWater` (356), `RenderRain`
(330), `ApplyOne` (320). Each is one feature's whole frame, so they are long rather than tangled. Split them when
next touched (`RenderSmoke`: the simulation, the lighting, the draw).

### 8. Duplicated helpers (partly fixed, a8c0327)

Smoothstep was written six times under five names. It is now one `Smoothstep` in `internal.h`. Luma is still
written by hand (`0.3f * x[0] + 0.59f * x[1] + 0.11f * x[2]`) in 32 places, and the sun-height computation in 4.

### 9. randy-vk.ini written twice by hand (diagnostic added, 389f9a5)

The user's ini had `RVK_CloudNight` twice (0.4 and 0.8). Only the first counts, so editing the second did
nothing. randy-vk.log now names such keys at start.

### 10. Docs drift (partly fixed)

Many plan docs still say "planned" or "plan" for work that has shipped (`clouds-layer-plan.md`,
`clouds-sheets-plan.md`, `clouds-types-plan.md`, `sky-weather-plan.md`, `storm-plan.md`, `stability-plan.md`,
`canopy-plan.md`), and several have no status line (`desert-plan.md`, `native-ground-plan.md`, `pebbles-plan.md`,
`terrain-plan.md`). **Recommendation:** one status line per plan (built in which version, or open), and a short index
in `docs/rvk.md`. (Done on the way: the six released plans and the native ground's and pebbles' got a dated status
line. Still to check: `canopy`, `render-batching`, `desert`, `terrain`.)

### 11. Branches and worktrees (left to the user)

There are 9 worktrees and 4 branches not merged into main (`light-groups`, `pbr-leaves-test`, `pr9-test`,
`pr9-ui-custom`; the last three are test merges). **Recommendation:** decide on `light-groups`, then delete the test
branches and the worktrees of merged work (`git worktree list`). Nothing was deleted in this audit.

### 12. No regression check for the look (done: `tools/look-check.py`)

Every visual change is checked by eye in the demo and then in game. A tuned value changed by mistake goes unseen
until a player reports it. **Recommendation:** a look check. It would run a handful of fixed demo scenes (a dusk sweep,
a night, a storm, the native ground) and compare region averages with stored numbers, with a tolerance above the
run-to-run noise (which is about ±0.002 in region luma; single pixels vary by up to ~60/255 between runs because of
the water and dithering, so whole-image diffs don't work).

Built on the way: `tools/look-check.py` with `tests/look-baseline.tsv`. It runs six scenes in about 15 s. Its
first run found a validation warning in every rainy scene, now fixed: the near rain's layer drew with a shader
writing five targets into a pass with one.

## What was checked and found sound

- Compiler warnings: 9 in a clean build, all now fixed.
- Settings: every one of the 319 reaches the renderer; the window's hidden list, the presets and the vanilla values are
  consistent.
- `ThreadedDevice`: the methods that touch the device directly (`Init`, `Resize`, `ReadPixels`, `SetWindow`, the
  state lists) are the synchronised ones.
- Device members: the three that look unused are read in `rvk.h`'s inline methods.
- The demo's validation runs: only the known `vkDestroyDevice` leak messages.
